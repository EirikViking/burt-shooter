# Nova Swarm Early Pilot Overhaul: The Swarm Got Teeth

Publish date: 2026-06-12

Early pilots did exactly what early pilots are supposed to do: they flew into the machine, came back smoking, and told us where the bolts were loose.

The verdict was useful and a little rude, which is the best kind of useful. Nova Swarm had the fast arcade spine. It had the cabinet energy. It had the bones of a game that could eat a whole evening and leave fingerprints on the glass. But it needed more spectacle, more progression, more readable chaos, and a stronger reason to hit One More Run before pretending to be a stable citizen.

So this update goes hard.

## The Cockpit Is Clearer

![Main menu run explainer](D:/vibe-coding-e/nova-swarm-sector-continue-prototype/test-results/smoke-2026-06-12T16-48-13-997Z/01-menu.png)

The main menu now explains what the run modes actually mean. Launch Run is the ranked climb: progression, unlocks, leaderboards, the whole glorious paperwork explosion. Sector Start is practice from a chosen sector: useful, focused, and not pretending to be the main climb.

The Hangar now recommends the best unlocked ship before a run, because a ship you unlocked and forgot is not a ship, it is a museum exhibit with thrusters. The fullscreen option was removed from Settings, the Windows exit dialog is gone, and the duplicate combo readout has been cleaned off the HUD.

The How to Play screen also got rebuilt into something much sharper.

![How to Play overhaul](D:/vibe-coding-e/nova-swarm-sector-continue-prototype/test-results/how-to-play-2026-06-12T16-35-06-026Z/menu-how-to-play.png)

It is cleaner, more high-tech, less cramped, and no longer looks like a ship manual written during decompression.

## Bosses Now Have Friends, Which Is Terrible News

![Boss encounter](D:/vibe-coding-e/nova-swarm-sector-continue-prototype/test-results/smoke-2026-06-12T16-48-13-997Z/13-boss-active.png)

Boss fights are now much more alive. Bosses can call in 111 different support ships that help them survive. These ships are not meant to be invisible punishment. They are bright, readable priority targets. If you ignore them, the boss gets breathing room. If you delete them, you get that clean arcade joy of solving a problem with forward motion and unreasonable lasers.

Boss 1 is now Sonia. Boss 3 is now KurtBossEdgar. Their Codex stories have been expanded too: Sonia gets a long sci-fi romance with tragic heat-shield energy, and KurtBossEdgar gets a Jeppe paa Berget-inspired descent into grand delusion, cosmic status anxiety, and the sort of leadership that makes engineers hide in vents.

And when bosses die now, they die properly.

![Boss defeated](D:/vibe-coding-e/nova-swarm-sector-continue-prototype/test-results/smoke-2026-06-12T16-48-13-997Z/14-boss-defeated.png)

The old jokey death delivery is out. Boss deaths now pull from 100 dramatic male agony voice lines. No quips. No wink. Just theatrical pain from a bad man having a very educational afternoon.

## The Swarm Is Bigger From The Start

![Gameplay polish](D:/vibe-coding-e/nova-swarm-sector-continue-prototype/test-results/smoke-2026-06-12T16-48-13-997Z/02-gameplay.png)

The enemy roster has exploded.

There are now 1,423 additional unique enemy profiles available from level 1. That means early runs should feel less repetitive without being turned into a blender. The aim is variety, movement, color, and surprise while keeping patterns readable enough that failure still feels like your fault, which is the sacred contract.

After level 10, 177 extra mayhem ships join the pool. This is where Nova Swarm is allowed to start looking like the universe lost a bet. More colors. More explosions. More weird ships screaming across the lane. More late-run energy without simply twisting the difficulty knob until it snaps off.

The 58 dangerous mid-ship variants still appear after sector 8, bridging normal enemies and elites. They are the middle managers of violence: not quite bosses, not quite cannon fodder, absolutely convinced they are important.

## The Leaderboard Got Ambition

Leaderboards now support Top 40 throughout the game. If fewer players exist, you see fewer. If the board is full, it has room to breathe.

Top 10 placements get special ceremony: stronger visuals, dedicated fanfare, and a more heroic presentation while staying readable. The goal is simple. If you claw into the top shelf, the game should act like the cabinet noticed.

Progression is bigger too. There are now 40 pilot ranks, including 20 new hard ranks with sharper lore and Codex coverage. Pilot Ranks also hydrate properly in the Codex during live runs, so the tab no longer looks empty when you have clearly been out there doing expensive things to hostile spacecraft.

## More Reasons To Run Again

Sector Start "One more run" now restarts from the same sector instead of sending you back to Sector 1. That one matters. A runback should feel like the game is holding the door open, not moving the building.

Ten small easter eggs now appear across runs. They are little signals from the machinery. Some are jokes. Some are warnings. Some are probably evidence in a future inquiry.

Level clears now draw from a 200-line voice pool. The game has more celebration, more texture, and more little hits of encouragement between disasters. Music was not replaced or altered.

## Codex Got Fed

The Codex now covers the expanded enemy roster, the 111 boss support ships, the full 40-rank ladder, and the new boss stories. It should feel less like a checklist and more like the black box recovered after a beautiful mistake.
